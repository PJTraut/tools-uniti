## The problem in one paragraph

J1 made `\Justification Left` and `Right` the physical edges (`AlignAbsolute`). Paratext's and PTXprint's stylesheets don't mean that: their directional values are logical, so a stock `usfm.sty` or `custom.sty` used in a right-to-left book gives the wrong result in UNITI. Underneath, UNITI never decides a paragraph's direction. It lets Qt guess from the first strong character of the visible text, so a Standard-view paragraph (which starts with a Latin marker tag) is always left-to-right, and a Preview paragraph that starts with Latin text or only digits flips. The project's own direction setting is never read.

## What was checked first

All measured 2026-10-15, PySide6 6.11.2 (Qt 6.11), offscreen, with the real `block_format`, the real Standard and Preview projections, and the Arabic project `arNAV12` (a Paratext project mounted on `/Volumes/Win11Arm64`).

1. **The built-in `usfm.sty` does use `Left` and `Right`.** The J1 "As built" note says it has none. `\Justification Right` is on `\ipr`, `\pr`, `\cls`, `\pmr`, `\qr`, `\lit` and the `thr*`/`tcr*` cell styles; `Left` is on `\s3`, `\s4` and `\sp`. Before J1, Qt turned those around in a right-to-left paragraph; since J1 `\qr` sits on the start edge like every other line, and `\s3`, `\s4` and `\sp` jump to the left of a right-aligned book.
2. **PTXprint's stylesheet geometry is logical in a right-to-left book.** The [PTXprint release notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sillsdev/ptx2pdf/master/docs/inno-docs/ReleaseNotes.txt): 1.7.9 (2021-05-20) "The TeX macros have been changed for RTL for Right and Left Justification such that they are swapped … If justification is Left, it will be Right-justified"; 2.2.30 "Fix RTL aligned (not justified) text (for example, \qr)". The user's account of the mechanism is that PTXprint typesets everything left-to-right and flips the result in XeTeX, which makes every directional stylesheet value start- or end-relative. The mechanism is the user's; the justification swap is documented; that margins mirror too was an inference, since confirmed from the macro source (point 13).
3. **Margins and first-line indent disagree in Qt.** `leftMargin` and `rightMargin` are physical; `textIndent` and a plain alignment follow the paragraph's direction. Stock `\q1` (`LeftMargin 0.75`, `FirstLineIndent -0.5`) in an RTL paragraph: the first line sits flush at the right edge (gap 0, clipped) and the continuation lines get no indent at all, because the 0.75" lands on the empty left side. In LTR the first line is 29 px in and the rest 77 px in, as intended.
4. **Direction is whatever Qt's first-strong rule gives.** Standard: all four blocks of a Hebrew sample were left-to-right (`\p …`, `\q1 …`, `\qr …`, and `\c 1`). Preview: right-to-left only when the text starts with an RTL letter or a digit; `Paul שלום עולם` is left-to-right, and the chapter heading `1` is too. The rich views read nothing of the plain-text view's Auto/LTR/RTL override.
5. **The project declares its direction and UNITI doesn't read it.** `arNAV12/Settings.xml` has `<LeftToRight>F</LeftToRight>`. `paratext_project.py` reads `Name`, `FullName`, `LanguageIsoCode`, `Encoding`, `Versification`, `StyleSheet`, `Guid`, `BooksPresent`, the file naming and the word affixes.
6. **PTXprint's own direction is per print configuration, not per project.** `arNAV12/shared/ptxprint`: `Mark`, `John` and `Luke` have `ifrtl = Right-to-Left`, `script = Arab`, `mirrorlayout = False`; `Matthew` and `NewArabicVersion` have `ifrtl = Left-to-Right`, `script = Zyyy`, `mirrorlayout = True`; `Default` has `ifrtl = ltr`. They disagree with each other, so the Paratext `LeftToRight` flag is the project-level source and the PTXprint configurations are not read.
7. **Marker tags scramble in an RTL paragraph.** Under an explicit RTL base, Standard's `\p \v 1 …` renders as `p \v 1\`. Wrapping each marker token in LRI…PDI (U+2066, U+2069) fixes it: the markers read `\p \v 1` from the right.
8. **Isolates work in Qt and cost nothing.** LRI, PDI, LRE, PDF and FSI are honoured; an isolate adds no width (75 px with and without). In an Arabic paragraph, `Paul,` renders as `,Paul` (the comma jumps to the wrong side) and as `Paul,` inside LRI…PDI. Brackets and digits were already right.
9. **Inline Latin in Arabic is rare, and authors already mark it in the source.** `arNAV12`: 69 books, 41,095 lines; 3 lines mix Latin and Arabic, all front matter. The source holds 277 LRM, 62 RLM, 5 LRE and 6 RLE characters, which Qt honours as written. PTXprint also reads those characters (1.8.3 "Fix handling of Grk/Hbr embedded within RTL/LTR text"; 2.2.35 "Added RLE,LRE,PDF…") but has no stylesheet field for a direction.
10. **Justification itself is fine.** `Both` fills lines in RTL and puts the last line on the start edge; an unset `\Justification` sits on the start edge.
11. **UNITI is screen-only and has no browser engine.** Standard and Preview are a `QTextEdit` (`rich_view.py`) filled from the projection; the plain-text editor paints itself over Qt's text shaping; the Markdown view is a `QTextBrowser`. No QtWebEngine, no `setHtml`. HTML's `dir`, `<bdi>` and logical margins are therefore not available on screen, but they are exactly what an export can use.
12. **There is no HTML export, and the pieces for one exist.** The only export of the rich views is the `.sty` one (`stylesheet_support.py`, an `EnvironmentCommand` returning `SaveTextFile(title, default name, text, filters)`). Preview's projection is Qt-free and already carries everything an export needs: the text, a block per paragraph with `align`, margins, `first_indent`, `space_before`, `hyphenate` (and `direction` after W2), spans with their flags, size and colour, and the notes section. Colours in the projection are the stylesheet's raw `#rrggbb`; the readable-on-theme adjustment happens only in `rich_view.char_format`.
13. **PTXprint's margins and first-line indent are logical in a right-to-left book** (read 2026-10-20 in [ptx2pdf](https://github.com/sillsdev/ptx2pdf) `master`). The "flip" is XeTeX's bidi extension: `paratext2.tex` sets `\TeXXeTstate=1`, and an RTL paragraph's text goes inside `\beginR` (`ptx-para-style.tex`, `s@tsideskips`, line 540). TeX--XeT reverses the text inside `\beginR…\endR`, while `\leftskip` and `\rightskip` stay on their physical sides. So the macros swap explicitly, in the same routine (lines 536–570, commented "inside \beginR rightskip is leftskip and leftskip is rightskip!"): with `\ifRTL`, `\LeftMargin` is added to `\rightskip` and `\RightMargin` to `\leftskip`, and `Left` puts its stretch in `\leftskip`, which makes the lines flush right. That matches the 1.7.9 note ("If justification is Left, it will be Right-justified"). The first line's indent is a cutout on the right in RTL (`\rightcutout`, line 870), so `\FirstLineIndent` is start-side as well. Qt's `textIndent` already behaves this way. A PTXprint PDF is no longer a gate. It stays as a by-hand check in the live-app checklist.

## Decisions

- **W-D1: the stylesheet's directional values are logical, as in PTXprint.** `Left` and `Right` are the start and end edges of the paragraph's reading direction; `\LeftMargin` and `\RightMargin` are the start-side and end-side margins; `\FirstLineIndent` is the start-side indent. In a left-to-right paragraph nothing changes. `Start` and `End` stay as synonyms of `Left` and `Right` (clearer in a rule meant for both directions). This reverses J1's "physical" decision; the stock stylesheets and Paratext projects then behave as authored. A physical edge isn't offered: nobody has asked for one.
- **W-D2: where a paragraph's direction comes from, highest first.** (1) `\Direction` on its style or a rule (W-D4); (2) the view's Auto / Left-to-Right / Right-to-Left choice, the same one the plain-text view has, saved per view; (3) the project's `LeftToRight` setting; (4) the first strong character, as today. A file outside a project has no setting, so it keeps 4 unless the user chooses a direction for the view.
- **W-D3: the direction applies in Standard and Preview, including the notes frame.** The projection carries a `direction` attribute on each block; `block_format` sets the block's layout direction from it, so alignment, indent and the margins resolve against a known direction and not Qt's guess. In an RTL paragraph, Standard puts each marker tag in an LRI…PDI isolate (generated segments), so source markers keep reading `\p \v 1`.
- **W-D4: `\Direction LTR|RTL|Auto` on paragraph styles and rules.** A UNITI field, read from any layer but meant for `uniti.sty` (Paratext and PTXprint ignore unknown fields, and PTXprint has no equivalent). `Auto` means "use W-D2's lower sources". It's for exceptions, such as English front matter in an Arabic project. Registered as a known presentation field, so the Style Inspector shows it.
- **W-D5: character direction is Unicode isolates, optional and last.** Qt has no per-character direction, so `\Direction LTR|RTL` on a character style makes the projection put a generated LRI or RLI before and a PDI after the span. The source map skips them, as it does soft hyphens (H-D5), and copy leaves them out. Only built if a project asks for it (see the evidence in point 9); authors who need it today put LRM or LRE in the source, which already works.
- **W-D6: no automatic isolation of Latin runs.** It would be a display-only heuristic that could be wrong; not planned.
- **W-D7: PTXprint's `ifrtl` and `mirrorlayout` are not read** (point 6).
- **W-D8: Preview can be exported as HTML, from the projection, not from Qt.** A Qt-free serializer in core writes the paragraphs, spans and notes the Preview shows, with the stylesheet's logical values kept logical in CSS (`dir`, `text-align: start|end`, `margin-inline-start|end`, `text-indent`, `<bdi>`, `hyphens`). It does not use `QTextDocument.toHtml()`, whose output is physical and Qt-specific. This needs the projection to keep margins logical (see W3). Preview only: Standard is the source, and its tags are not publishable text.

### W1: Read the project's direction (core, Qt-free)
`ParatextProject.right_to_left: bool | None`, from `Settings.xml`'s `LeftToRight` (`F` is right-to-left, `T` left-to-right, missing or unreadable is `None`). W8 also needs the project's `DefaultFont` (`ParatextProject.default_font: str | None`), read in the same place; `Language` and `LanguageIsoCode` are already read. Tests with a temporary project folder: `F`, `T`, missing, garbage, and the Paratext 8+ and older layouts.

**As built (2026-10-15).** `ParatextProject.right_to_left` and `default_font` in `paratext_project.py`. `LeftToRight` `F`/`false` is `True`, `T`/`true` is `False`, anything else (missing, empty, garbage) is `None`, case-insensitive; `DefaultFont` is trimmed, blank is `None`. Both are read from the same `Settings.xml` root for either naming layout, nothing else changed. Checked on the real `arNAV12`: `right_to_left=True`, `default_font='Adobe Arabic'`. Nothing reads them yet (W2 and W8 do), so no view changes. Tests in `tests/test_paratext_project.py`.

### W2: Block direction in the projection
`layout_projection(..., direction=…)` sets `direction: ltr|rtl` on every block (and the notes frame's blocks) by W-D2's order: style or rule `\Direction`, then the view's choice, then the project. No source means no attribute (Qt's first-strong, unchanged). `block_format` calls `setLayoutDirection` when the attribute is there. The rich views read the same per-view Auto/LTR/RTL choice as the plain-text view, and the projection cache key includes it. Tests: a tag-led and a Latin-led Hebrew paragraph, a digit-only heading, and the chapter heading `\c`, all take the project's direction; no attribute, no change.

**As built (2026-10-15).** `layout_projection(..., direction=)` (`"ltr"` or `"rtl"`, anything else is none) puts a `direction` attribute on every block, `\id` and other unformatted ones included, and on the notes frame's blocks; a block's own `direction` would win (W5 supplies it). The text and source map are unchanged. Both SFM views got `project_with(source, hidden_categories, hyphenation, direction)`; Standard ignores the first two, and `project_filtered` stays. `ProjectionCache.project` takes `direction` and keeps one projection per value. `block_format` sets the block's layout direction, and the empty line after a final line break takes the last block's. `RichViewPane.set_direction`; `RichViewController.direction_for(view)` gives the tab's Auto/LTR/RTL choice, else the project's `LeftToRight`, else none. It is applied when a pane is shown, when the choice is changed, and when the window is activated (so a `Settings.xml` changed in Paratext is picked up). The status-bar note for the direction in effect (Risks) is not done. `Left`, `Right`, margins and indent are still J1's until W3, so an RTL project's `\q1` and `\qr` are now laid out against a known direction but with the physical edges W3 replaces. Tests: `tests/test_sfm_direction.py`, `tests/ui/test_rich_view_direction.py`.

### W3: Logical `Left`/`Right` and margins (reverses J1)
`block_format` maps `left` and `right` to the plain flags (no `AlignAbsolute`) and `start` and `end` the same; `block_format` swaps `left_margin` and `right_margin` when the block is right-to-left, from the block's `direction` attribute (W2) or, when there is none, from the first strong character of the block's text (`render_projection` passes `block_format` the resolved direction). The projection itself keeps the margins logical, start-side and end-side as the stylesheet wrote them, so the HTML export (W8) can write them as `margin-inline-start` and `margin-inline-end` without undoing a swap. `FirstLineIndent` is left as is. ~~Gate: question 1~~ (answered, point 13). Tests: replace `test_left_and_right_are_physical_in_either_direction` with the logical one; stock `\qr`, `\s3`, `\sp`, `\pr` and `\q1`/`\li1` hanging indents in RTL (first line outdented into the start-side margin, continuation lines indented), and unchanged output in LTR.

**Implementation notes (2026-10-20).**
- `rich_view.block_format(attrs, direction=None)`: `left` and `start` become `AlignLeft`, `right` and `end` become `AlignRight`, with no `AlignAbsolute`. When the resolved direction is RTL, `left_margin` goes to `setRightMargin` and `right_margin` to `setLeftMargin`. `setTextIndent` is unchanged (Qt already puts it on the start side). The comment above it cites W-D1 and point 13, not J-D1.
- The resolved direction is the block's `direction` attribute. If the block has none, `render_projection` asks Qt what it decided (`QTextBlock.textDirection()` after the text is in), not `core.bidi.is_rtl_paragraph`: point 4 found Qt takes a digit-led paragraph as right-to-left, which that function doesn't, so only Qt's own answer matches the alignment Qt applies. The empty line after the final break needs no margins, so it is unchanged.
- `views._ALIGN`'s comment and `projection.py`'s `align` note: `left`/`right` are the start and end edges; `start`/`end` are synonyms. The values themselves stay as they are, so the HTML export (W8) can still tell what the author wrote.
- Tests go in `tests/ui/test_rich_view_alignment.py` (painted text, as J1's are), using the real `usfm.sty` records for the stock cases. Also: a block with no `direction` whose text starts Arabic gets its margins swapped, and one that starts Latin doesn't.
- Docs land with the phase: the [USFM Stylesheet Rules](../../usfm-stylesheet-rules.md) Justification section (line 156: "`Left` and `Right` are the physical edges" is reversed) and its margin rows; the STATUS J1 line (no longer a known issue); the J1 "As built" note gets "superseded by W3" (moved here from W7, since it's the same change).

**As built (2026-10-20).** As the notes say. `block_format(attrs, *, right_to_left=False)` sets both margins whenever either is given, so the second merge for a block with no `direction` (in `render_projection`, for each Qt block whose `textDirection()` is right-to-left) can't leave a stale one behind. The tests in `tests/ui/test_rich_view_alignment.py` paint the stock `usfm.sty`'s Preview: `\q1` hangs ¼" and ¾" from the right, `\qr` sits left and `\s3` right in RTL, both with the project's direction and with none. They also cover a Latin-led paragraph with no direction and unchanged LTR output. The RTL tests fail on the J1 code. Docs: the stylesheet reference's Justification section and margin rows, the USFM page, STATUS, the live-app checklist (section 13) and J1's "As built".

### W4: Tags in Standard stay readable
In a right-to-left Standard paragraph the marker tokens get a generated LRI before and PDI after. Tests: the source map round-trips every offset, Find and copy take source text only, the text with the isolates removed equals today's, an LTR paragraph gets none, and the caret reaches each marker (the arrow keys step over a zero-width character; if they stall, fix with the source map as H-D5 did).

**Implementation notes (2026-10-20; question 2 answered: Standard right to left).**
- `layout_projection`: with `show_markers`, `tag()`, `visible_token()` and the milestone tag write `builder.generated("⁦", "isolate")` before `builder.marker(…)` and `builder.generated("⁩", "isolate")` after it, but only when the effective direction is RTL. That is the open block's `direction` attribute (W5's style value), else the view's `direction`; before the first block (`\id`) it is the view's. The `marker_token` span covers the label only, so the isolates take no tag background.
- Isolates are generated segments, so `view_to_source` maps them to the tag's edge, as with soft hyphens. Typing just before and just after a tag must land in the same source offset as today. Test that directly; it's the riskiest part.
- Copy: `_RichEditor.createMimeDataFromSelection` always strips view-only characters, not just when Hyphenate Preview is on. One constant in core (`VIEW_ONLY_CHARACTERS`: U+00AD, U+2066–U+2069) is shared by the strip and by W8's serializer.
- Spelling and Find use source offsets through the map, so they need tests only: an underline next to a tag doesn't widen, and Find's highlight of a word next to a tag stays on the word.
- Tests: `tests/test_sfm_direction.py` (projection: isolates only around RTL tags, text minus isolates equals the LTR projection, every source offset round-trips) and `tests/ui/test_rich_view_direction.py` (painted order `\p \v 1` read from the right, caret steps, copy).

**As built (2026-10-20).**
- `layout_projection` puts the isolates around every tag it shows (`shown_tag`): Standard's tags, milestones, and the undefined markers both views keep visible. A Preview of an RTL book therefore isolates `\zz`-style unknown markers too. Note bodies in Preview's notes frame are not isolated (their unknown markers are rare, and the notes' builder has no block direction to read). The direction is the open block's own `\Direction` (W5), else the view's.
- `source_map.py` holds the shared names: `LRI`, `PDI`, `ISOLATE` and `HYPHEN` (Preview's soft hyphens now use the constant), `VIEW_ONLY_MARKERS`, and `SourceMap.view_only_positions(start, end)`.
- **A mapping bug the tests found:** `source_to_view` looked only one segment back, so the end of the word before a tag mapped to just *inside* the tag's isolate (`יהוה⁦`). That would have widened spelling underlines and Find highlights by one. It now steps back over a tag's opening LRI, and only that, so soft hyphens and Preview's generated line breaks map as before. An offset at the second of two adjacent tags lands between their isolates.
- The pane keeps `_view_only` (view positions of generated soft hyphens and isolates) and `_isolate_stops` (the Qt positions just inside an isolate). After any navigation key, `step_past_isolates` moves on past a stop in the direction the caret went, keeping a selection's anchor. Without it, each tag cost two extra presses that left the caret where it already was. Backspace and Delete take a view-only character together with the one beside it, so a press never only steps over an isolate. Copy drops the view-only characters by position, not by value, so soft hyphens and isolates written in the source itself are kept; it replaces Hyphenate Preview's strip-by-value.
- Tests: in `tests/test_sfm_direction.py`, isolates around every RTL tag (not inside the tag's colour), none in LTR or undirected paragraphs, per paragraph by `\Direction`, every source offset round-tripping as without them, Preview's visible unknown marker, a word beside a tag mapping onto the word alone, and two adjacent tags. In `tests/ui/test_rich_view_direction.py`, glyph positions read `\p \v 1` from the right, the arrow keys step past every stop and reach every other position, typing beside a tag (and just inside an isolate) lands as before, Backspace, and copy. The painted, typing, Backspace and copy tests fail without the isolates; the arrow test fails without the step.

**As built (2026-10-20).** As the notes say: `formatting_attrs` reads `\Direction` (trimmed, any case) for blocks only, so a character style's `\Direction` is ignored until W6. ### W8: Export Preview as HTML

If the command context doesn't carry those, it is given the tab's Preview settings the way `project_with` receives them.
- **Serializer.** `sfm/html_export.py`, Qt-free: `export_preview_html(projection, *, title, language, direction, font) -> str`. UTF-8, `<meta charset>`, everything escaped; one `<style>` block with a class per distinct block and span attribute set (each element also gets `data-marker="q1"`, so the file can be restyled), no inline style except where a `p+q1`-style rule changes a block.

  | Projection | HTML and CSS |
  |---|---|
  | project direction | `<html lang dir>`; blocks with `direction` get `dir`; a block without one gets `dir="auto"` (the browser's first-strong, as Qt's Auto) |
  | `align` `left`/`start` | `text-align: start`; `right`/`end` → `text-align: end`; `center`, `justify` as written |
  | `left_margin`, `right_margin` | `margin-inline-start`, `margin-inline-end` in `in` |
  | `first_indent` | `text-indent` |
  | `space_before`, `space_after` | `margin-block-start`, `margin-block-end` in `pt` |
  | bold, italic, underline, small caps, superscript | `font-weight`, `font-style`, `text-decoration`, `font-variant: small-caps`, `<sup>` |
  | `font_scale`, `color` | `font-size: …em`; the stylesheet's own colour, not the editor theme's |
  | `hyphenate` | `hyphens: manual`; the soft hyphens go out as `&shy;` |
  | character `\Direction` (W6) | `<bdi dir>` around the span |
  | table rows (`\tr`) | `<table>`: one `<tr>` per row, a `<td>` per cell, header cells as `<th>` |
  | notes | the caller as `<sup><a href="#n1" id="r1">`, the notes after the text as an `<ol class="notes">` with a back link; categories show in brackets, as in Preview |
- **Font.** `Settings.xml`'s `DefaultFont` (arNAV12 has `Adobe Arabic`), read with the other project fields in W1's reader, goes first in `font-family`, then `serif`; the file embeds no fonts.
- **Generated text.** The projection's generated segments (the `\cat` brackets, cell tabs, callers, soft hyphens) are already in the projection text, so the export needs no source map. The U+00AD and any isolate characters are written as entities or elements, never raw.
- **Tests (core).** A golden file per direction: a Hebrew or Arabic paragraph, a left-to-right one, a stock `\qr`, `\s3` and hanging `\q1` in RTL (start-side margin and `text-align: end` in the output); escaping of `<`, `&` and `"`; soft hyphens; a table; notes with a hidden category; colour (raw, not theme-adjusted); the output parses with `html.parser` and the text content equals the projection's. **Tests (window).** The command offers `SaveTextFile` with the Preview's filter applied. **By hand:** open an `arNAV12` export in a browser and compare it with Preview (the live-app checklist entry); the browser's bidi engine is an independent check of W2–W4.
- **Not in the export.** The Standard view, page layout and pagination, printing, fonts, and the verse and chapter numbers' own styles beyond what the stylesheet gives them (they follow `\v` and `\c`'s styles like any span).

**Implementation notes (2026-10-20; written for question 4's proposed answer, which was then answered "whole book": see As built).**
- **A host feature, not a plain `EnvironmentCommand`.** `EnvironmentContext` carries the document, path, caret and anchor, but not the tab's hidden categories, Hyphenate Preview rules, direction or chapter window. So the USFM environment declares `EnvironmentCommand("export_preview_html", "Export Preview as HTML…", group="styles", host_feature="sfm.export_preview_html")`, the same pattern as Load Stylesheet…, and the window implements it. The `SaveTextFile` is built host-side and passed to `EnvironmentController.save_text_file(view, result)`, which already does the dialog and the atomic write.
- **Host side** (`ui/preview_export.py`, owned by the window like `ChapterView`): it takes the tab's Preview projection from `PROJECTIONS.project(document, preview_provider, source, view.hidden_note_categories, hyphenation.rules_for(view), RichViewController.direction_for(view))`. That is the same cache key the Preview pane uses, so it costs nothing when Preview is already showing, and it works from Standard or Plain Text too. The window is `view.source_window()` when Show One Chapter is on. Project fields (`language`, `default_font`) come from `project_for_file(view.document.path)`. The default name is `<document stem>.html`, saved next to the book. The Preview right-click menu gets the same action through `_context_entries`.
- **Serializer** (`core/environments/sfm/html_export.py`): `export_preview_html(projection, *, title, base_size, language=None, direction=None, font=None, window=None) -> str`. `base_size` is `body_font_size(styles)` and becomes `body { font-size: …pt }`, so `font_scale` writes as `em`.
  - *Blocks*: one `<p>` per block (USFM blocks carry no `heading_level`; headings are styled `<p>`s, as in Preview). Each gets `data-marker` and a class per distinct attribute set (`b1`, `b2`…). A `p+q1` rule simply yields another attribute set and class, so no inline style is needed (this simplifies the plan's "inline style where a rule changes a block").
  - *Runs*: the same boundary sweep as `render_projection`. Each run is a flat `<span class="s3 s7">` of the spans over it (no nesting to balance), and marker tokens (an unknown marker Preview keeps visible) get `class="marker"`. Superscript is `vertical-align: super; font-size: smaller` in its class, not `<sup>`, so runs stay flat.
  - *Window*: a block is written when `source_map.view_to_source(block.view_start, prefer_left=False)` is inside it (the rule `show_blocks_in` uses), and note entries likewise, so the file matches what Preview shows.
  - *Notes*: Preview's caller span gets a `note` attribute (its kind) in `move_note_to_frame`. `char_format` ignores it, and it costs one more attribute set. The n-th caller of kind `f` in the text links to the n-th entry with a caller in the `f` section (`id="f-3"`, back link `#f-3-ref`). An entry whose note was written with `-` has no caller and no link.
  - *Tables*: consecutive `table_row` blocks form one `<table>`. The generated tabs split the row into cells, and a cell whose span marker starts `th` becomes `<th>`.
  - *Characters*: `html.escape` for everything; U+00AD becomes `&shy;`, and the source's own LRM/RLM/LRE/RLE/PDF and any isolate go out as numeric entities. `VIEW_ONLY_CHARACTERS` (W4) comes from the same constant.

The text-content check removes the generated callers' links but keeps the callers. **As built (2026-10-20).** As the notes say, with these differences:
- **Scope:** always the whole book (question 4's answer). No `window` parameter; Show One Chapter is ignored.
- **The command** is in its own `export` group at the end of the USFM menu. It is on Preview's right-click menu only, not Standard's. A book above 1 MiB isn't exported (Preview doesn't show one either); the status bar says why. The host is `ui/preview_export.py` (`PreviewExport`, `window._preview_export`). It projects through `PROJECTIONS` with the tab's filter, hyphenation and direction, so an open Preview's projection is reused. `_SfmViewBase.styles` was added for `body_font_size`.
- **Callers are paired through the source map,** not a new `note` attribute on the caller span: existing tests pin the caller span's attributes, and the source already orders the pair. A caller's generated segment sits at its note's opener, and the first note body at or after that offset, across every kind, is its own. Note entries are found by line, not by block: without a direction a note's paragraph has no attributes, so the projection records no block for it. That was found by the first test run.
- **Runs:** one `<span>` per run with the spans' attributes merged as `render_projection` merges them (marker tags reset). Sizes are `rem` (the root is `\p`'s size), and superscript is `0.7em`, so nesting never compounds. A note entry without a block takes its kind as `data-marker`.
- **Tests:** in `tests/test_sfm_html_export.py`, the two golden pages, logical CSS in RTL (no physical `left`/`right` anywhere in the style block), `dir="auto"` without a direction, text content equal to the projection's (`html.parser`), escaping of text, title and font name, `&shy;`, a hidden category, callers paired across `\f` and `\x` with a `-` caller left unlinked, a table with `<th>`, a raw stylesheet colour, and an undefined marker. In `tests/ui/test_preview_html_export.py`, the menu and context-menu entries, an export of an RTL project (`lang`, `dir`, font, title, both chapters, filter applied) with Show One Chapter on, and no export for a plain-text tab.

## Risks

- **Qt's flags are the mechanism for `Left` and `Right`** (a plain alignment is mirrored by the block's direction). An explicit direction makes that deterministic, but a future Qt could change the behaviour: the alignment tests use real painted text, as J1's do.
- **Caret and selection over generated isolates** (W4, W6): the same family of problem soft hyphens had; the source map is where it's solved.
- **A direction chosen for the view and the project disagreeing** is intended (the view wins), but the status bar should show the direction in effect.
- **The HTML export is a second renderer of the same projection.** A browser's justification, hyphenation and bidi differ from Qt's in small ways, so the export is faithful to the stylesheet, not pixel-identical to Preview; golden tests pin the HTML, not the look.
- **PTXprint's margin behaviour is read from its macros, not seen on a page** (point 13). The reading agrees with the 1.7.9 release note and with how TeX--XeT treats `\leftskip` and `\rightskip`, but a PDF from an RTL configuration is still the direct check (live-app checklist).

## Out of scope

- Reading PTXprint's configurations (`ifrtl`, `mirrorlayout`, `script`).
- Automatic isolation of Latin runs inside RTL paragraphs (W-D6).
- The plain-text editor's own direction handling, which already has its override and isn't changed.
- Mirrored page layout (inner and outer margins, binding side): UNITI has no pages.
- Shaping, kashida and digit substitution.
- Exporting to PDF, ePub or any paged format, and embedding fonts in the HTML.
