# UNITI v0.001a22 Dogfood / Performance Alpha Design

Date: 2026-09-05
Status: approved
Milestone: [`v0.001a22` — Dogfood / Performance Alpha](v0.001a22-dogfood-performance-alpha.md)

## Goal

Measure and harden sustained real-world UNITI use without broadening the editor's product scope. A22 must expose accumulation, race, recovery, and performance defects before beta while preserving the text-integrity, bounded-resource, one-service, one-document-authority, and cross-platform guarantees implemented through A21.

A22 adds little product behavior. It extends the existing performance system, records bounded content-free dogfood evidence, and corrects structural defects revealed by that evidence.

## Approved decisions

1. A22 uses a hybrid gate. Numeric regression thresholds are authoritative only on a controlled compatible reference host; hosted cross-platform runs gate correctness, cleanup, bounded growth, hangs, and completion deadlines rather than raw timing.
2. Sustained tests use deterministic cycle counts with wall-clock safety deadlines, not time-only soak runs.
3. One process-lifetime `UNITIService` is reused within each workload family and restarted between families. This reveals within-session accumulation while isolating unrelated failures.
4. Hosted runs execute five cycles per family. Controlled reference-host runs execute fifty cycles per family.
5. Canonical performance evidence retains one approved anchor plus at most one candidate for each compatible measurement profile. Generated corpora and raw run artifacts are disposable.
6. Comparative timing regression requires two clean compatible controlled runs. Integrity loss, hangs, leaks, unbounded growth, and recovery failures fail immediately.
7. Memory, CPU, disk-capacity, durability, and worker pressure are injected through policy seams. Tests measure real bounded workload use but never exhaust the host or manufacture LOWDISK.
8. Crash and recovery testing terminates isolated children at deterministic named publication phases and verifies recovery from harness-owned roots.
9. Normal application use is recorded locally and always on. The recorder stores only bounded aggregate metrics and outcome codes, never document content or identifiers.
10. Dogfood evidence is retained for the first of seven days or 16 MiB. Nothing is uploaded automatically.
11. A22 requires seven separate active dogfood days before freeze, with no minimum-hour requirement.

## Existing baseline and gaps

The A21 baseline already includes:

- thirteen isolated quick and routine user-experience scenarios covering Open, navigation, typing, scrolling, giant lines, sparse and dense search, replacement, regex intelligence, Save, Save As, resource recovery, and session restore;
- deterministic ordinary, Unicode, giant-line, sparse-search, dense-search, mixed-EOL, malformed-UTF-8, and sparse-file corpus generation;
- quick 10 MiB, routine 100 MiB, and sparse 1 GiB design-target tiers;
- host fingerprinting, capacity preflight, controlled versus real-world modes, native Qt facts, and compatible-baseline checks;
- centralized absolute responsiveness, cancellation, memory, pressure, and comparison policy in `performance_policy.toml`;
- isolated child execution with deadlines and bounded stdout/stderr capture;
- schema-versioned JSON results, human reports, and selected A18/A19 baseline evidence;
- an application-wide `ResourceManager`, cancellable task coordinator, bounded disposable cache, immutable document snapshots, and stale-result rejection;
- one process-lifetime `UNITIService`, one authoritative `Document` per source identity, bounded saved Undo/Redo, durable session generations, recovery journals, and explicit Quit; and
- a fail-closed four-lane macOS, Windows, and Linux CI gate with bounded seven-day failure artifacts.

The existing point scenarios intentionally restart for each repetition. They can prove individual operations but can hide service-lifetime memory, handle, task, result-store, and temporary-file accumulation. They do not repeatedly combine Open, edit, search, replacement, Save, close, and recovery in one service. Current comparison logic also makes one compatible run authoritative, and the application does not yet retain bounded real-use performance evidence.

## Scope

A22 includes:

- deterministic sustained workload families and cycle orchestration;
- repeated use of one service within a workload family;
- warm-up and bounded checkpoint sampling;
- exact per-cycle integrity and ownership checks;
- retained-memory, handle, task, document, spool, and temporary-artifact growth evaluation;
- two-run confirmation for comparative timing failures;
- one-anchor/one-candidate baseline selection and retention;
- deterministic pressure, cancellation, external-edit, crash, and publication-race coverage;
- an always-on, local-only, content-free dogfood aggregate recorder;
- bounded recorder publication, pruning, export, diagnostics, and clearing;
- five-cycle cross-platform hosted coverage and two fifty-cycle controlled reference runs;
- native-GUI and sparse design-target evidence; and
- structural correction of defects discovered by the approved evidence.

## Outside A22

A22 does not add syntax highlighting, projects, plugins, LSP, Git integration, terminals, cloud telemetry, accounts, remote upload, automatic baseline selection, remote crash reporting, installers, signing, notarization, automatic updates, or a permanent installed daemon.

It does not create a general event analytics system, retain raw editor events, collect document or expression content, introduce randomized fuzzing as a freeze gate, or deliberately stress the host through real disk filling, unbounded allocation, CPU saturation, or uncontrolled process creation. Executable health/recovery and native bundling remain A23 concerns; polished installers, signing, and updating remain outside the queue.

## Approaches considered

### Selected: extend the existing performance system

The existing corpus, runner, host-preflight, result, evaluator, report, and packaged-policy components gain a sustained-workload layer. The production recorder shares schema vocabulary and policy definitions but never imports benchmark workload control or fault orchestration.

This preserves one measurement language, one safety policy, and one compatibility model while adding the least new machinery.

### Rejected: separate endurance harness

A separate harness would isolate endurance code, but it would duplicate or bridge host fingerprinting, corpus generation, result evaluation, reporting, and policy. Two partially overlapping performance authorities would increase drift before beta.

### Rejected: application telemetry as the primary harness

Driving acceptance mainly from production recording would resemble real use, but it would make exact corpora, phase faults, cycle counts, cleanup checks, and reproduction weaker. Production evidence supplements deterministic tests; it does not replace them.

## Architecture

```text
performance_suite.py
        |
        v
parent run coordinator
  |-- host/capacity preflight
  |-- policy + profile selection
  |-- one owned corpus/root at a time
  |-- child deadline + bounded artifacts
  |-- aggregate/evaluate/report
  |
  +--> point-scenario child (existing behavior)
  |
  +--> sustained-family child
          |-- one UNITIService
          |-- one warm-up cycle
          |-- 5 or 50 measured cycles
          |-- bounded checkpoints
          |-- exact integrity/cleanup facts
          `-- final teardown result

normal UNITI process
        |
        v
service-owned dogfood recorder
  |-- fixed counters and histograms only
  |-- low-priority bounded publication
  |-- 7-day / 16-MiB local ring
  `-- explicit sanitized export
```

The parent owns preflight, corpus creation, process isolation, timeout enforcement, artifact budgets, aggregation, and cleanup. A sustained child owns exactly one application service and cannot select or overwrite a baseline. Benchmark-owned roots are resolved explicitly; cleanup never targets a workspace, repository root, home directory, or unresolved environment path.

The result and policy schemas advance compatibly. Readers continue to accept existing schema-1 point baselines. New sustained records identify their workload profile, cycle count, warm-up count, checkpoint policy, fault schedule where applicable, and measurement vocabulary version. Unknown fields or incompatible schema/profile/host facts prevent comparison rather than producing a false pass.

## Workload families

### Daily editing and navigation

Each cycle opens an owned ordinary document, performs bounded typing and deletion, moves and selects before and after the cursor, exercises Find Previous and Find Next without requiring Find All, runs Find All, uses Undo/Redo, saves, closes the view, and reopens the document through the same service.

The cycle verifies logical text, revision, cursor-relative navigation, save point, exact output digest, document-registry uniqueness, shared-history authority, result freshness, and expected state after close/reopen.

### Format and byte integrity

Deterministic UTF-8, mixed-Unicode, mixed-EOL, BOM, and malformed-byte fixtures exercise Open, format inspection, controlled display-only invalid-byte handling, explicit conversion where requested, Save, Save As, history restoration, and external-edit detection.

The exact saved-byte SHA-256 authority decides whether saved history remains compatible. Metadata can avoid unnecessary hashing but never overrides a detected content mismatch. No test silently normalizes bytes, BOM, encoding, or line endings.

### Search, regex, replacement, and cancellation

Sparse matches, dense matches, zero-width results, captures, bounded lookarounds, and approved pathological expressions exercise direct previous/next navigation, Find All, report/capture generation, result spooling, cancellation, replacement-plan admission, Replace, Replace All, and atomic Undo/Redo.

Patterns are fixed test fixtures and run under the existing context, memory, timeout, and cancellation bounds. A test that expects refusal verifies the structured refusal; it never waits for uncontrolled engine work.

### Session, recovery, and lifecycle

Cycles create multiple documents and independent views, publish durable sessions and saved histories, close and reactivate windows while the service remains alive, restore active-first/lazy state, resolve recovery choices, and perform explicit Quit.

The family checks one service, one global Find/Replace surface, one authoritative document per source, exact saved histories, independent view state, session-generation authority, recovery-journal cleanup, and absence of orphan storage or worker tasks.

## Corpus and cycle policy

The existing point tiers remain authoritative for 10 MiB quick, 100 MiB routine, and sparse 1 GiB characterization. Sustained families use smaller physically bounded fixtures, no larger than the quick-tier size, so fifty lifecycle repetitions do not turn into an accidental disk-throughput soak. One family corpus is materialized at a time and removed only from its exact owned root after the child has exited.

Every family performs one unmeasured warm-up cycle, then five hosted or fifty controlled-reference measured cycles. Cycle counts are policy values, not literals distributed through tests. Each operation and child has a parent-enforced deadline. A deadline cancels or terminates only the isolated child and preserves bounded diagnostics for evaluation.

Cycle order, corpus seed, edits, searches, replacement plans, cancellation points, and fault points are deterministic and recorded in the profile version. The controlled suite does not adapt its work based on measured speed, because adaptive cycle counts would make comparisons ambiguous.

## Measurement model

Point-scenario measurements remain unchanged. Sustained families add fixed checkpoint observations after warm-up, during the measured sequence, and after final teardown. With five cycles, the early and late windows use the first and last two measured checkpoints. With fifty cycles, they use the first and last ten. Stored samples are capped by the configured cycle maximum; the recorder and runner never create an unbounded event list.

Measurements include, where meaningful:

- operation completion and GUI-heartbeat latency;
- cancellation and first-progress latency;
- search and replacement throughput;
- current, peak, and retained process RSS;
- process handle or descriptor count when a reliable platform probe exists;
- registered documents, open views, active/queued tasks, cache use, result stores, replacement plans, snapshots, and owned temporary paths;
- output digests, revisions, save points, history depth, session/recovery generation outcomes, and cleanup state; and
- unavailable-probe facts rather than invented zero values.

Correctness facts are evaluated before performance. A fast operation with incorrect bytes, stale state, or incomplete cleanup fails.

## Gate model

### Immediate gates

Any integrity loss, stale result application, ambiguous publication authority, incomplete recovery, unexpected child exit, deadline, orphan task, leaked owned document/result/plan/snapshot/temp artifact, or failed required cleanup is an immediate failure on every host.

Owned-resource counts must return exactly to the profile's declared post-cycle or post-teardown state. Process-level handle counts can vary because of platform and runtime internals, so warning/failure allowances live centrally in policy and apply only when a reliable probe is available. Probe unavailability is explicit and cannot weaken owned-resource checks.

### Growth gates

Late-window retained RSS is compared with the early post-warm-up window. An increase of 16 MiB warns and 32 MiB fails. The inherited absolute retained-growth failure limit of 64 MiB remains authoritative. The evaluator uses window medians so one allocator or collector sample does not decide the result.

A positive trend below the warning threshold remains diagnostic. Repeated stepwise growth in owned counts fails even if RSS stays below its threshold. Stable reusable cache after warm-up is allowed only when it remains within the existing resource-manager budget and is released at final teardown.

### Timing and comparison gates

The controlled compatible reference host retains the existing 15% regression warning and 25% regression failure thresholds. A comparative failure becomes authoritative only when the same metric reaches the failure threshold in two clean controlled runs from the same candidate commit and compatible profile. One such run is a provisional warning pending confirmation.

Absolute GUI-freeze, interaction, cancellation, progress, and memory limits remain immediate safety gates. Two-run confirmation applies only to comparative timing noise, never to an absolute safety or correctness failure.

Hosted raw timings and throughput are reported as diagnostics. Hosted lanes still fail on child deadlines, hangs, absolute unresponsiveness that prevents completion, correctness, cleanup, and bounded-growth gates.

### Result states

- `PASS`: every required invariant and applicable budget passed.
- `WARN`: a warning threshold, unconfirmed comparison regression, or reviewable trend was observed.
- `FAIL`: an immediate gate, absolute limit, or confirmed comparative regression failed.
- `INVALID`: contention, incompatible fingerprints, interrupted measurement, or unavailable required controlled evidence prevents a valid comparison.
- `NOT RUN`: safe capacity or required capability was unavailable before workload creation.

`INVALID` and `NOT RUN` never become passes. Required freeze evidence must be rerun on an eligible host.

## Baseline policy

An approved anchor is immutable evidence for one schema, workload profile, tier/mode, host fingerprint class, native/offscreen mode, and source milestone. A candidate is a bounded proposal, not current truth. Tooling never promotes, rewrites, or deletes the anchor automatically.

At most one approved anchor and one candidate are retained for each compatible measurement profile. A candidate record can contain both clean confirmation runs; it does not require two permanent raw result files. Replacing an existing candidate is explicit and recoverable through Git or the user's chosen output location.

The first A22 sustained anchor has no older sustained comparison. It requires two same-commit controlled runs whose metrics agree within the warning envelope and satisfy all absolute, integrity, growth, and cleanup gates. Selection is an explicit reviewed change. Later candidates compare against the anchor, and a replacement requires an explicit architectural or performance justification rather than accepting regression as a new normal.

Committed anchors contain aggregate samples, host facts, policy/schema versions, source commit, and evaluation outcomes. They contain no corpus, editor content, path, expression, stdout/stderr stream, or raw event log.

## Always-on dogfood recorder

The process-lifetime `UNITIService` owns exactly one recorder. It starts with the service, remains active when the last window closes, and ends at explicit Quit. Opening another window or forwarding from a secondary launch does not create another recorder.

The recorder accepts only a fixed, low-cardinality vocabulary:

- approved operation identifier and outcome category;
- fixed latency histogram bucket and operation count;
- peak and retained resource bands;
- cancellation, timeout, recovery, durability, and external-change outcome codes; and
- build identifier, OS family, and coarse host class.

It never stores document text or bytes, paths, filenames, source identities, search or replacement expressions, clipboard content, window titles, arbitrary labels, exception text, stack traces, or dynamic keys. There is no document correlation identifier. Nothing is transmitted automatically.

Events update bounded in-memory counters and fixed histograms. The GUI thread performs no dogfood file I/O or compression. A low-priority service task publishes at a bounded cadence, daily rollover, and orderly Quit. Save, session, and recovery durability always outrank recorder publication. Losing recent unpublished dogfood counters during a crash is acceptable; delaying or weakening editor durability is not.

## Recorder storage and controls

Recorder storage is limited to seven days or 16 MiB, whichever is reached first. It contains at most one immutable compressed segment per completed local day plus one bounded current segment. Safe atomic-publication helpers write segments. Oldest complete segments are pruned after a successful rollover or before an admitted publication would exceed the aggregate cap; the current segment is never allowed to grow without bound.

A publication failure sets a bounded recorder-unavailable diagnostic and does not enter the recovery journal, block Quit, or retry without limit. Recorder storage is not saved document history and cannot consume its 256 MiB union budget.

`Export Dogfood Evidence` explicitly writes one schema-validated, sanitized aggregate file selected by the user. `Clear Dogfood Evidence` removes only resolved recorder-owned segments after explicit user action. Export does not select a baseline or initiate network activity.

An active dogfood day is a local calendar day whose aggregate records at least one document open, one authoritative edit transaction, and one save, discard, or close lifecycle outcome. A22 freeze requires seven such separate days plus a brief content-free checklist confirming coverage of ordinary editing, large-file use, format/invalid-byte inspection, regex/search/replacement, and recovery/lifecycle workflows. No minimum hours, filenames, or document contents are recorded.

## Pressure and race injection

Resource tests inject immutable host snapshots, capacity results, durability outcomes, task states, and write/sync failures through existing dependency seams. Normal, Busy, Constrained, and Critical transitions, recovery hysteresis, cache eviction, worker admission, cancellation, refusal, and recovery are therefore deterministic.

The harness measures real bounded workload RSS and process resources but never allocates memory, fills a filesystem, reserves free space, spins CPUs, or creates uncontrolled workers merely to move a host probe into distress. LOWDISK remains synthetic. The existing 512 MiB free-space reserve and tier-specific capacity preflight remain mandatory.

Race tests use named barriers instead of timing sleeps. The controller can pause a child immediately before or after result publication, revision validation, destination identity validation, session/recovery manifest publication, current-pointer publication, recovery compaction, and final cleanup. It then performs the deterministic edit, close, cancel, external write, pressure transition, Quit request, release, or termination.

Test-only controllers are supplied explicitly to the relevant service; the production default is a no-op. There is no global hidden environment switch that can crash a normal editor process.

## Crash and recovery testing

Crash cases operate only inside a resolved harness-owned application root and on owned destination files. The parent waits for a named phase barrier, terminates the isolated child, then starts a clean verifier process against the same owned root.

Covered phase families include:

- save staging, flush, sync, identity recheck, atomic replacement, and post-commit verification;
- session pack, manifest, and current-pointer publication;
- recovery append, transaction seal, compaction snapshot, journal replacement, and cleanup; and
- explicit Quit while session or recovery publication is pending.

The verifier proves that either the previous complete generation or the newly complete generation is authoritative, never a mixture. It verifies exact bytes and hashes, revision/save-point state, saved Undo/Redo compatibility, recovery choices, absence of partial published manifests, and bounded cleanup.

An external-edit case changes an owned source between recorded metadata and the next edit/save/history action. SHA-256 over exact saved bytes remains authoritative. Stale saved history is rejected, and the external version is not overwritten without the existing explicit conflict workflow.

Expected injected termination is evidence setup, not a failure. Missing prior evidence, ambiguous authority, corrupted replay, an unexpected exit, a hang, or leaked owned artifacts fails.

## Artifact and disk budgets

- Only one sustained family corpus is materialized at a time.
- Each family result is capped at 2 MiB decoded; the complete suite result is capped at 8 MiB decoded.
- Successful raw stdout, stderr, phase traces, corpora, and owned temporary trees are discarded after the compact result is validated.
- Failure evidence is parsed, sanitized, bounded, and retained for seven days under the existing CI discipline.
- Selected anchors and one candidate contain aggregates only.
- Dogfood segments have their independent seven-day/16 MiB cap.
- Capacity refusal occurs before large corpus or stage creation.
- No test creates real LOWDISK state.

A result that exceeds its evidence budget fails safely and preserves only the bounded reason. Truncation can never remove or rewrite the gate outcome into a pass.

## Error handling and cleanup

- A failed child does not prevent independent later families from running, but the suite remains failed.
- Parent timeouts terminate only the exact child and retain bounded diagnostic facts.
- Host contention invalidates comparative timing but does not erase correctness findings.
- Incompatible baselines are reported and ignored for comparison; they are never coerced into compatibility.
- Recorder failure remains visible in diagnostics without affecting editing, saving, session publication, recovery, or Quit.
- Cancellation closes snapshots, result stores, replacement plans, byte sources, and stages before the next cycle checkpoint.
- Final family teardown closes windows, documents, workers, cache entries, the service, and recorder tasks in defined order.
- Cleanup paths are resolved beneath the exact owned root before removal.

## Verification strategy

Ordinary automated tests cover:

- schema-1 compatibility and new sustained/recorder schemas;
- policy relationships, cycle limits, evidence caps, and unknown-field rejection;
- deterministic family profiles, corpus seeds, warm-up, checkpoints, and cycle counts;
- exact text/byte/revision/save-point/history/result integrity for every family;
- service reuse inside a family and isolation between families;
- early/late window aggregation, RSS growth, owned-resource equality, probe availability, and handle allowances;
- immediate versus two-run comparative gate semantics;
- anchor/candidate compatibility, first-anchor selection, and non-automatic promotion;
- recorder fixed vocabulary, content/path exclusion, cardinality bounds, background publication, rollover, seven-day/16 MiB pruning, export, clear, and failure isolation;
- injected pressure without host exhaustion or real LOWDISK creation;
- named cancellation, edit, close, external-change, publication, crash, and Quit barriers;
- previous/new complete generation recovery after child termination;
- bounded artifact handling and exact-root cleanup; and
- human and JSON reports that cannot hide `FAIL`, `INVALID`, or `NOT RUN`.

The hosted matrix keeps the A21 lanes:

| Lane | Sustained responsibility |
|---|---|
| macOS / Python 3.12 | complete correctness gate plus five cycles per family |
| Windows / Python 3.12 | complete correctness gate plus five cycles per family |
| Linux / Python 3.12 | complete correctness gate plus five cycles per family |
| Linux / newest stable Python | complete correctness gate plus five cycles per family |

Hosted timing is diagnostic. The controlled reference host runs two clean fifty-cycle suites on one candidate commit. Native GUI evidence covers interactions that offscreen Qt cannot faithfully characterize. The sparse 1 GiB design-target probe runs once and remains separate from sustained repetition.

## Milestone acceptance

A22 freezes only when:

1. the complete ordinary automated suite is green;
2. all four hosted lanes pass the same source commit with five sustained cycles per family;
3. two clean compatible controlled reference runs pass fifty cycles per family;
4. one reviewed A22 anchor is selected without retaining raw corpora or unbounded evidence;
5. the sparse 1 GiB design-target probe passes without whole-file allocation;
6. native GUI evidence satisfies applicable absolute safety gates;
7. seven separate active dogfood days and the content-free coverage checklist are recorded;
8. every immediate integrity, recovery, cleanup, timeout, and growth gate passes;
9. every comparative warning is reviewed and no repeated 25% regression remains;
10. full compile, deep self-check, offscreen/native smoke, and documentation-coherence checks pass; and
11. no known data-loss, text-integrity, stale-result, runaway-growth, unrecoverable-publication, or basic responsiveness defect remains.

Required `INVALID` or `NOT RUN` evidence blocks freeze until rerun on an eligible host. A push required for hosted evidence needs separate authorization. A22 completion does not itself authorize a tag, release, installer, bundle, or package.

## Documentation boundary

Until verified, A22 behavior remains in `02_plans`. Current architecture, scope, status, and development records continue to describe the implemented A21 baseline. Verified workstream records may move to `03_implemented` without completing the parent milestone.

At A22 completion, the milestone, design, and execution plan move to `03_implemented`; current status, scope, architecture, development guidance, roadmap, implemented index, and handover advance coherently to A23 Executable Health & Recovery Alpha.
